# Product vision

Treasure Trap is a live multiplayer cartoon pirate party quiz. Friends sail as a fleet,
race toward answer islands, collect treasure, bluff, mutiny, use outrageous pirate tools and
survive high-drama special rounds. It should feel like a purpose-built game, never a quiz
website with a pirate skin.

## Audience and tone

- 2–8 players on separate phones/laptops, together in a room or voice call.
- Suitable for ages 9+, with cheeky, dramatic and funny party-game energy.
- Rules are learned through symbols, staging and short animated demonstrations.
- Table discussion is part of the game: persuade, bluff, coordinate and betray.

## Product pillars

### 1. A cartoon pirate excursion

The dominant fantasy is a fleet travelling through a lively storybook sea. Ships bob, sails
snap, waves move, islands approach and threats emerge from the world. Pirate symbolism should
make actions self-explanatory: sail to an answer, fire a cannonball, raise a white flag, walk
the plank, mutiny against the captain.

### 2. Selective casino excitement

Casino language is an **intensity tool**, not the global skin. Use slot-machine anticipation,
lights, escalating ticks, rarity colour, near-miss timing and payout fanfare for:

- loot-box/chest opening (the primary casino moment);
- jackpot item awards;
- selected high-stakes reveals inside special rounds;
- exceptional payouts where the contrast makes them feel valuable.

Do not cover ordinary sailing and questions in neon casino chrome. Contrast is what makes the
reward moments exciting.

### 3. Deception through discussion

`Imposter`/social-spy-style uncertainty is a major inspiration. Mechanics should create useful
things to say and reasons to doubt them. Secret mutiny is the first locked expression: players
can claim they will mutiny, bluff that they know the answer, or bait others into forfeiting.

### 4. Knowledge usually wins

Trivia ability should be the strongest predictor of victory (target hypothesis: roughly 75%).
Items and wildcard events create comebacks and stories, but must not turn the winner into a
random draw. Scores never go below zero and attacks cannot erase a whole game.

### 5. Mario Kart-style items

Items are immediately readable, fun to fire, position-aware and capable of changing a moment
without making skill irrelevant. Lower-ranked players have better access to stronger tools.
Every hostile item needs warning/readability, a limit or counterplay, and a bespoke fleet-world
animation.

### 6. One polished slice at a time

The first special round is Million Pound Drop / Loot Drop. Make its complete multiplayer loop,
interaction, reveal and audiovisual payoff excellent before adding another special round.
Breadth must not come at the cost of game feel.

## Non-goals for the playtest prototype

- Accounts, payments, public matchmaking or a database.
- A native app before the web loop is proven.
- Twelve shallow special rounds built simultaneously.
- Casino imagery on every screen.
- Static cards, generic forms or unexplained icon soup during gameplay.

## Product success

Remote friends can open a web address, create/join a room, understand what to do without a
rules lecture, complete a stable game, talk and bluff naturally, and immediately want another
run because the outcomes looked and sounded memorable.
